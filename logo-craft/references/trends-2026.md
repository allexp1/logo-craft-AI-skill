# Trends 2025 to 2026, and how to use them

Know the trends so you can avoid dating a mark, and so you can adopt the *system* behaviours (dark mode, motion, layered icons) that are not fashions but platform facts. Observed, not endorsed. Refreshed via `.living/KNOWLEDGE.md`; the LogoLounge report appears each spring.

Contents: 1 what the reports say · 2 platform facts that look like trends but are not · 3 the "blanding" correction · 4 the AI aesthetic · 5 motion · 6 how to use all this

## 1. What the trend reports say

**LogoLounge 2026 Logo Trend Report** (Bill Gardner): fifteen categories drawn from a review of more than 30,000 submitted logos and identity systems. Named categories include *Slip Knots* (interlaced ribbon forms), *Slant Break* (letters or bars interrupted along a single diagonal), *Faders* (forms dissolving into dots or steps), *Colorforms* (flat overlapping colour fields), *Open Axis* (shapes split along an open axis) and *Phasers* (stepped or phased repetitions). Broader observations: motion as a design dimension, atmospheric gradients, expressive interventions in letters, dimensional rendering, and configurable systems. Gardner is explicit that the report describes what designers are doing, and is not a recommendation to imitate.

**LogoLounge 2025**: vivid colour, animation, and a tension between technological and restorative (natural, hand-made) aesthetics. The same caveat applies.

Other widely observed 2024 to 2026 patterns: the return of serifs and character in wordmarks; heritage revivals (Burberry 2023, Pepsi 2023); flat "glyph" symbols reduced to one shape; variable and kinetic type; monochrome marks designed for themed icons; and a flood of near-identical "AI" identities (next section).

## 2. Platform facts that look like trends but are not

These are requirements, not fashions. Adopt them in every identity:

- **Dark mode variants** of the mark, favicon and icon (iOS dark/tinted appearances, Android themed monochrome icons, `prefers-color-scheme` favicons).
- **Layered icons**: iOS 26/27 Icon Composer renders Liquid Glass from flat layers; Android renders masks from foreground/background layers. Flat artwork, no baked effects.
- **Responsive levels**: lockup, symbol, compact glyph; the switch sizes written down.
- **Maskable/circular safety**: avatars and PWA icons are cropped to circles; design for it.
- **Motion-ready construction**: a mark built from a few parts can be animated (Google dots, Pepsi pulse); a traced illustration cannot. This is a construction choice, not a trend.

## 3. The "blanding" correction

Between roughly 2015 and 2021 many brands converged on the same geometric sans wordmark (Burberry, Balmain, Saint Laurent, Google, Airbnb, Pinterest, Spotify). Critics called it blanding: distinctiveness traded for a generic "digital" neutrality. The correction since 2022: Burberry's return to serif lettering and its knight (2023), heritage-informed marks, more expressive letterforms. Lesson: reduction can strip character along with detail; keep one memorable property. This does not mean serifs beat sans; it means character beats its absence.

## 4. The AI aesthetic

The 2023 to 2026 wave of AI products converged on: four-point sparkles, purple-to-teal or iridescent gradients, glowing orbs, swirls and knots, brains with circuits, hexagonal flowers. These are now a category cliché and several resemble the major labs' marks (which are trademarks). An AI product that wants to be remembered in 2027 should look like a specific, calm, human tool: a real letterform, a flat colour nobody in the category uses, a metaphor from the actual job rather than from "intelligence". Avoid sparkles entirely unless the product is literally about stars.

## 5. Motion

Motion identities are now expected for digital brands (launch animations, loading states, social stings). Design for motion by construction: a mark of two to five parts with a clear relationship (circles that meet, bars that slide, a stroke that draws itself) animates naturally. Always approve the **static** mark first and ship it as the master; motion is an expression of the identity, never its substitute. Keep a reduced-motion variant in mind (a fade) for accessibility.

## 6. How to use all this

- Before the concept checkpoint, look at the three candidates and ask: "which of these will look like 2026 in 2031?" Faders, slant breaks, atmospheric gradients and sparkles are the answer today.
- Adopt every item in §2 regardless of taste.
- Use trends as a vocabulary to describe what a client likes ("they like the slant-break feel") and then find the timeless version of it (one diagonal in the letters, no fashionable finish).
- Record in the handover which trend, if any, the mark participates in and why it will survive it.
