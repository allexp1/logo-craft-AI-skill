# Industry conventions and the cliché watchlist

Every category has a visual convention its audience has learned, and a set of clichés its designers have exhausted. Know both: the convention is what you decide to keep or break; the cliché is what you avoid unless you can say in one sentence why yours is different. Brand names below are real companies cited as examples; never imitate their marks.

Contents: 1 how to audit a category in ten minutes · 2 conventions by sector · 3 the cliché watchlist · 4 what to do with a cliché you cannot avoid

## 1. Audit a category in ten minutes

1. Web-search "<category> logos" and "<category> app icons" and look at the image results; also look at the five to eight competitors named in the brief.
2. For each, note in one line: mark type, dominant colour, shape family (circle, square, swoosh, letter), type class, mood.
3. Write the **convention** ("blue, geometric sans, abstract rounded symbol") and the **openings** (nobody uses a serif; nobody uses warm colour; everyone's icon is a gradient blob).
4. Decide, in the brief: conform (category recognition matters more, e.g. a bank wanting trust) or depart (distinctiveness matters more, e.g. a challenger). Write the reason.
5. Keep the list; you will present it on the concept sheet as the shelf the mark must stand on.

Logobook (monochrome symbols by category), Brand New (redesign reviews) and LogoLounge are useful references for this step. They are catalogues of what exists, never sources to trace from.

## 2. Conventions by sector

| Sector | Convention (what the audience expects) | Exhausted clichés | Openings and notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Banking, fintech, payments** | Blue/navy or green; geometric sans; abstract stable symbols (shields, columns, circles); flat and calm | Shield + check, upward arrow/graph, coin stack, handshake, globe, infinity, padlock | Warm colour and lowercase wordmarks (Monzo, Wise); a single bold letter; patterns of ledger lines; trust from craft rather than from a shield |
| **Developer tools, infrastructure, cloud** | Dark mode first; monospace or grotesque; hexagons, cubes, nodes, brackets, terminals | Hexagon, cube/isometric box, `</>` and `>_` glyphs, network of nodes, cloud outline, gear, rocket, lightning bolt | A real letterform with one mechanical detail; a single geometric mark that is not a hexagon; colour nobody in the stack uses |
| **AI and ML products** | Purple-to-teal gradients; sparkles, stars, brains, neural nets; soft blobs | Sparkle/four-point star (the 2023 to 2026 AI icon), brain with circuits, glowing orb, swirl, eye | Calm, specific, human: a letter, a tool metaphor from the actual job, flat colour. Avoid anything that could be mistaken for the big labs' marks (hexaflower, swirl knot, spark) |
| **B2B SaaS** | Geometric sans wordmark + abstract rounded symbol; blue or indigo; friendly | Checkmarks, chat bubbles, rounded squares with a cut, three ascending bars, "people" dots | A character-rich wordmark; a monogram with a real idea; an unexpected hue |
| **Consumer social, messaging** | Speech bubbles, cameras, hearts, waves; saturated single colour; rounded | Chat bubble, heart, camera, play button, @ and # | Abstract gesture, a letter as the symbol, bold colour field |
| **Kids and education** | Warm saturated primaries; rounded sans; mascots, stars, pencils, books, owls, light bulbs | Owl, light bulb, pencil, open book, graduation cap, apple, rainbow, smiling letter, brain | A real character with a story; a letter that is a toy; a shape that becomes a game element; keep legibility for parents |
| **Health, medtech, pharma** | Blue/teal/green; crosses, hearts, leaves, pulse lines, caducei; clean sans | Cross, heartbeat line, caduceus, hand holding figure, leaf+drop, DNA helix | Human warmth without a heart icon; a precise geometric symbol; a photographic-free palette with one warm accent |
| **Wellness, fitness, mindfulness** | Greens, earth tones, lavender; lotus, leaves, circles, running figure | Lotus, meditating figure, lotus-in-circle, leaf, zen circle (enso), running silhouette, flame, dumbbell | Typographic confidence; a single abstract form with breath or rhythm; black and one colour |
| **Food and beverage, restaurants** | Emblems, scripts, warm colour, illustration; appetite colours (red, orange, yellow) | Fork+knife, chef hat, wheat, coffee bean with steam, cocktail glass, tomato, chili | A crafted wordmark (lettering), a mascot, a bold single-colour badge that still has a compact version |
| **E-commerce and retail** | Friendly lowercase wordmarks; smiles, bags, carts, arrows; orange and red | Shopping bag, cart, smile arrow (Amazon owns it), price tag, box | A wordmark with one memorable letter; a symbol from the product category, abstracted |
| **Real estate, proptech** | Blue, green, gold; rooflines, houses, keys, skylines, pins | House outline, roofline chevron, key, skyline, location pin, door | A letter as a plan or a window; typographic restraint; a textured single colour |
| **Travel, mobility, logistics** | Blue, orange; planes, globes, arrows, roads, pins; italic motion | Globe, airplane, suitcase, compass, pin, swoosh, speed lines, arrow | Motion through a single angle in the letters; a route as a line; one unexpected colour |
| **Music and audio** | Black and vivid accents; waveforms, notes, headphones, play; rounded sans | Waveform, musical note, headphones, play triangle, vinyl record, equaliser bars, speaker | Rhythm in the type; a wave that is also a letter; monochrome with one neon |
| **Media, news, publishing** | Serifs, black, mastheads; strong wordmarks | Quill, newspaper, globe, megaphone | A typographic masthead with one customised letter; a monogram that works as a favicon |
| **Gaming and esports** | Aggressive display type, emblems, mascots, neon on black | Shield with wings, skull, dragon, controller, sword, lightning, "gamer" italic | A clean monogram or abstract symbol that survives as an avatar; restraint stands out |
| **Crypto, web3** | Hexagons, cubes, coins, chains, gradients, "Ξ"-like glyphs | Coin with letter, hexagon, chain link, cube, infinity, lightning | Plain typography and one honest colour; avoid finance clichés and crypto clichés at once |
| **Legal, consulting, finance services** | Serif, navy, gold, columns, scales, monogram emblems | Scales of justice, column, gavel, handshake, globe, mountain peak, compass | Modern serif wordmark with one crafted detail; a monogram with a real geometric idea |
| **Non-profit, community** | Hands, hearts, people-circles, trees, globes; green and blue | Hands cradling, people in a circle, tree, heart, globe, ribbon, dove | A distinct symbol from the mission's actual object; a strong wordmark; an honest colour |
| **Hardware, IoT, robotics** | Grotesque sans, black/grey + one accent; circuits, waves, eyes | Circuit lines, wifi arcs, robot head, eye, gear, chip | Precision geometry (one angle, one radius); a letter cut like a machined part |
| **Telecom, VoIP, connectivity** | Blue/purple/red; waves, signal arcs, globe, phone handset | Handset, signal bars, wifi arcs, globe, orbit rings, speech bubble | A letter whose counter is the signal; a rhythm of bars that is not the signal icon; warm colour |
| **Photo, video, creative tools** | Apertures, lenses, play, pens, brushes; black with gradients | Camera, aperture/shutter, play button, pen nib, brush stroke, colour wheel | Aperture is usable only if it is also a letter or a new geometry; a monogram; a bold flat field |
| **Security, privacy** | Shields, locks, keys, eyes, fingerprints; dark blue, black, green | Shield, padlock, key, eye, fingerprint, bug, fortress | Calm typography; a symbol of structure (a lattice, a knot, a fold) rather than a shield |
| **Pets, nature, outdoors** | Paws, leaves, mountains, trees, suns; greens, browns, oranges | Paw print, leaf, mountain range, pine tree, sun over horizon, compass | Animal silhouette drawn with one angle family; a letter as a landscape; restraint in colour |

## 3. The cliché watchlist

Not a ban: a watchlist. Each item requires a specific conceptual reason and a competitor comparison before it stays.

Globe · handshake · light bulb · upward arrow or bar chart · swoosh and orbit rings · hexagon and honeycomb · cube and isometric box · network of nodes · sparkle/four-point star · brain, brain with circuits · neural net · eye · shield, shield with check · padlock · key · checkmark · chat bubble · heart · house and roofline · location pin · tree and leaf · lotus and enso · drop · flame · lightning bolt · rocket · gear · magnifying glass · infinity · puzzle piece · people in a circle · abstract person with arms up · hands cradling · play triangle · camera and aperture · letter inside a circle with no further idea · three ascending bars · gradient blob · "AI" written with a sparkle · any letterform in Montserrat/Poppins bold with no modification.

## 4. What to do with a cliché you cannot avoid

Sometimes the brief insists (a coffee company that wants a bean, a school that wants an owl). Make it specific:

- **Change the level of abstraction**: the bean as a single tilted ellipse with one cut; the owl as two circles and one angle.
- **Merge it with the name**: the bean is the o; the roofline is the A.
- **Give it one unusual property**: an angle nobody uses, a counter that reads as something else, a proportion that is clearly chosen.
- **Pair it with a wordmark that carries the identity**, and treat the symbol as a secondary element.
- **Compare it on the shelf**: if it still looks like three competitors at 32 px in grey, the concept has not earned its place.
